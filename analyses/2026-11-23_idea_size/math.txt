# A new paradigm for mathematics.
 Why does the universe obey equations? The physicist Eugene Wigner called the usefulness of mathematics in physics "unreasonable" and said "there is no rational explanation for it" (Wigner, 1960). HMR has one. Mathematics is the ordered pattern of thought within a Universal Intelligence (UI), existing among thoughts without order. Nature is mathematical because it is made of that ordered thought.

## Version 2, November 23, 2026
### What was wrong with Version 1
It described thoughts and how they pile up, not thinking. Nothing in it could be true, false, or a lie, and it had no emotion and no memory. It also read one rule of steady growth as a candidate for inflation without testing whether what grows is a space.
### What Version 2 adds
Definitions of truth, mistakes, lies, and emotion. An exact answer to what one false part does to an idea, and what a contradiction costs, derived and not scored. Proofs checked by computer on every small pattern. Two negative results: Version 1's rules cannot remember, and no rule we tested holds a dimension. One positive: rules that copy shapes do remember.
### What we are trying to accomplish
One mathematics, built only from thoughts and the act of thinking, that gives a simple answer for matter, space, gravity, energy, time, and thought, and that is tested against what science has measured.
### Where we are falling short
How a thought comes to be “about” another is still open. The definitions are proved for single thoughts, not for human minds. No rule yet grows space, holds a memory in one place, or makes a particle. We predict no number that physics lacks, and we have made no progress on any famous open problem.

## One kind of thing, one kind of act.
 HMR says reality is Universal Intelligence (UI) thinking. If that is so, there should be a mathematics whose only object is a thought and whose only operation is thinking one. This section builds it from six assumptions, and every line carries a label that says how firm it is.
 So far the work reaches an exact family of rules for how one thought follows another. From there a task force is walking the model toward quantum mechanics, and toward one master equation. Version 2 adds a mechanics of meaning, and two results that say what the law still lacks.

### What HMR assumes
 Every theory starts from a few things it takes to be true and builds from there. These are HMR's. Everything else on this page follows from them or is labeled as something more.
 One intelligence. Reality is the thinking of one Universal Intelligence.
 One element. The element is a thought. A thought has no place and no size. All it has is how it relates to other thoughts.
 One relation. Thoughts happen one at a time, and each new thought builds on some of the thoughts before it. “Builds on” is the only relation there is. It never runs in a circle, and a thought rests on a finite number of others. Whether truth needs a second relation, “about”, or whether “about” can be derived from a law with memory, is open (the two routes).
 Only the pattern counts. If two thoughts are unrelated, it makes no difference which of them came first.
 No reaching across. What is thought next depends on what it builds on, and not on thoughts unrelated to it.
 Liking. Every possible next thought has some chance, and the chances are UI's liking: it does more of what it likes.
 Time is then the order of UI's thought, and space is what unrelated thoughts are to one another. The Physics page adds two assumptions of its own to reach measured numbers: that a very large pattern looks like space-time, and that one thought is one unit of volume.

### The rule for what is thought next
 Ask four things of any rule that gives the chance of each possible next thought:
 Nothing is born into the past.
 The chance of reaching a state does not depend on the order in which its unrelated thoughts were born.
 The odds between two possible births depend only on the two pasts involved, not on thoughts outside them.
 Something is always thought next: the chances add to one.
 If, in addition, no possible birth has chance zero, the rule has one form. There are numbers t0 = 1, t1, t2 and so on, none negative. In a state of n thoughts, a birth whose past holds w thoughts, m of them seen directly, has chance
 chance = λ(w, m) / λ(n, 0)λ(w, m) = Σk=m…w C(w − m, k − m) tk
 for each distinct past. C(a, b) counts the ways to choose b things from a. This is the classical sequential growth law of Rideout, and Sorkin (1999), found in the search for a theory of quantum gravity. established
 The simplest case
 Let each existing thought be attended to with the same chance p, independently of the others. Then the formula becomes
 pm (1 − p)n − w
 Start with one thought and p = ½. The second thought comes after the first with chance ½, and beside it with chance ½. That is the whole rule, at its smallest.
 The same rule, said as attention
 Pick a set S of existing thoughts, a set of k thoughts having weight tk. The new thought is born after S and all that S rests on. Checked by exact computation, on every pattern of up to five thoughts, by the mathematician and again by the referee: it gives the formula on the left. established Reading tk as UI's weighting of a focus of size k: HMR reading
 It is a family, not one law. The numbers tk are free, infinitely many of them, and nothing in the four conditions fixes them.

### Together is free. Apart is paid for.
 plinks × (1 − p)pairs apart
 This is the chance of a pattern grown in a given order. A link is a direct relation. A relation that follows from other relations costs nothing, and every pair of thoughts left unrelated costs a factor of 1 − p. So p is a strength of attraction: near 1 it draws thoughts into one line, near 0 it leaves them apart.

### Attention attracts attention

 This is the chance that the next thought builds on a given thought, where f is the number of thoughts already resting on it. To build on any of those is to build on it too. When p is small the chance is close to p × (1 + f), in proportion to what the thought has already drawn. That is “liking a thing, and so doing more of it”, with no liking put in.

### Two thoughts pull as a product

 This is the chance that the next thought comes after both of two thoughts whose followings are separate, where each pull is the chance in the card before. A product of two weights is the form of the top line of Newton's law of gravity. The bottom line, distance squared, is absent, because nothing in this rule knows distance.

 Liking is the mathematics of energy and of action. Give every pattern a cost:
 cost = links × ln(1/p)  + pairs apart × ln(1/(1 − p))
 The chance in the first card is then e−cost. Physics writes attraction the same way: at a fixed temperature a system is found in a state with chance in proportion to e−E/T, where E is the energy of the state, and a force is the slope of E. For a whole history physics uses one number in the same place, the action, and what happens is what makes the action least. In the general rule below, the chance of a step is a neutral chance times w(next) / w(now). A pattern of thoughts is a whole history, so −ln w plays the part of the action, and the pull toward a liked pattern is the slope of ln w.

 What the other three conditions allow. Fix any rule of the family, with all its numbers positive, as a reference. Then the rules that keep conditions 1, 2, and 4 are exactly
 chance = reference chance  × w(next state) / w(present state)
 where w gives every pattern a number that is never negative, w of the void is 1, and w of a pattern is the reference-weighted average of w over the patterns that can follow it. Here w is simply the chance of a pattern under the rule divided by its chance under the reference. It is a change of description, not a restriction, and it changes if the reference is changed. These rules are the order-invariant measures studied by Brightwell and Luczak (2011). established

 Version 1 could say how thoughts pile up. It could not say what a thought means, so nothing in it could be true, false, or a lie. ### Where does “about” come from?
 To be true, a thought must be true of something. Version 1 has one relation, “builds on”, and that alone does not pick out which earlier thought a thought is about. There are two routes, and we have not settled between them.
 Route 1: add it. Take “about” as a second relation beside “builds on”. Everything on this page then follows at once. The cost is a second relation in a model that had one.
 Route 2: derive it. A later pattern is a record of an earlier one when, under the law, its shape carries information about the earlier shape. Then “about” needs nothing added, but it needs a law that can remember.
 For the definitions below, reference is a partial map ρ from thoughts to thoughts. ρ(m) = t reads “m is about t”. It is grounded at m when ρ(m) ≺ m: the thought rests on what it is about. Under route 2, ρ would be the best matching under the law.
 open We are still working on it.
 Where route 2 stands. Version 1's rules cannot remember at all (the no-memory result). Rules in which a new thought copies an existing shape do remember: early and late shape then agree strongly, and shapes carry themselves forward in lines of descent. That is the raw material of a record. What is still missing is a record that sits in one place and can be rewritten (the copy-rule runs). If such a law is found, reference, memory, and a lasting particle would come from it together. HMR conjecture numerical for the runs

### Healthy and good

### Healthy

 Consistent. Its claims could all hold at once.
 Grounded. Every thought has a true chain down to something checked or to a stated assumption.

 Correctable. A thought shown false gets superseded, and balance returns quickly.

## What a false part does
 If an idea is built from smaller thoughts and one of them is false, can the idea still be wholly true? Or does one untruth at the bottom corrupt everything? The answer has three parts, and the damage can be counted exactly.
### 1. The whole is not wholly true
An idea that includes a false part is not 100% true, wherever the part sits. One false relation is enough.
exact in HMR given the definitions
### 2. What rests on the false part is unsupported, not false
Exactly the thoughts that rest on the false thought lose their warrant, and no others. Each may still be true for another reason, but this idea can no longer show it.
exact in HMR checked by brute force on every pattern up to 7 thoughts
### 3. What is supported another way stays true
A conclusion reached by a second chain that avoids the false part is still proved. A claim checked directly against what it is about is true in the range where it was checked, whatever its foundation.
exact in HMR checked by brute force on every pattern up to 7 thoughts
 The other half of the question also has an exact answer. If every smaller thought is truthful, the idea is truthful, and it says more than its parts: every relation that follows from the direct ones comes free. exact in HMR checked on 46,525 small cases. The one exception is joining two true ideas. The join is true only if both mean the same thing by what they share and every new crossing relation holds. Two true ideas can make a false one.

## Open problems
 Ranked by how much else waits on each.
### 1. Where “about” comes from
Everything about truth rests on one thought being about another. Two routes are open: take “about” as a second relation beside “builds on”, or derive it as a record under a law that can remember. Copy rules now give memory, which makes the second route possible in kind. Neither is settled. The two routes ↑ open
### 2. A memory that sits in one place
Version 1's rules have no memory: later shape is independent of earlier shape. Rules that copy shapes do remember, and shapes carry themselves forward. A memory that can be written and rewritten appeared only as one store for the whole pattern, kept by constant refresh. The next lead is a local store that is refreshed: growth that returns to the same neighborhood at a steady rate. The runs
### 3. Levels are unproved
A human thought is not one thought of UI. The order carries upward when a block counts as before another only if all of it is. The law is not known to carry, and in general a coarse law gains memory. Until this is settled, everything said here about human minds is analogy.
### 4. A part with its own liking
Which thoughts belong to one mind, and what that mind likes, are not defined. One candidate: a part's liking is UI's liking averaged over what the part cannot see, so agreement with UI would rise with knowledge. HMR conjecture
### 5. UI's own liking, and HMR's values in the mathematics
What UI likes is unknown in the mathematics. Good is defined by HMR's values. Writing them as mathematics is open, so good cannot be computed yet.
### 6. Approximate truth
As defined, truth is yes or no, and every physical law is an approximation. Claims need a tolerance and a range written into them before the definitions can be used on real science. Degrees of belief are also absent.
### 7. Meaning beyond order
Content here is only which thoughts rest on which. Qualities, “not”, and laws about all cases have no form yet. “The sky is blue” cannot be written.
### 8. The named emotions
Four numbers and one identity. Anger, grief, and fear are not derived.
### 9. No number yet
The first number this mathematics can produce is about HMR itself: build HMR's own graph of claims and report its false links, its contradictions, its suspects, and how well it is braced. Not yet built.

## The first boundary as a sphere: what is proved, and what is assumed.
 The Defining Universal Intelligence page says that once “me” has a reach, testing its edge in every direction traces a sphere, and with it π. The steps below show exactly what that takes.
 Given: a flat space of at least two dimensions, with distances that can take any real value; a point O (me); the unit d₀ (the first distance).
 Assumption (no hidden structure): the distance-preserving maps that keep O fixed can carry any point at distance d₀ to any other.
 Claim: any set of points at distance d₀ from O that is picked out by a rule using only O and d₀ is the whole sphere. In two dimensions its length is 2π d₀.
 Proof: a distance-preserving map that keeps O fixed keeps every statement written with O and distance true, so it carries the chosen set onto itself. The chosen set is then made of whole orbits of those maps, and by the assumption the sphere is a single orbit. ∎ established

### The fork: what is the first shape?
 If the only distance that exists is the unit, the edge of “me” can be one of three things. They differ in how the points on the edge relate to each other.
### Sphere
Points on the edge sit at every distance from each other, from almost nothing up to two units straight across. That needs distances which repeating the unit cannot supply, such as √2.
### Simplex
Every point is one unit from every other. Two points make a line, three a triangle, four a pyramid, and each further point needs a new dimension. There is no edge to measure and no π.
### Fan
Every edge point is one unit from me (gold), and the edge points relate to each other only through me. Any two are then two units apart: across, through the center.

## π: the unlimited, inside the first limit.
 π is the ratio of a circle's edge to its width. Three facts about it matter to HMR.
 It is forced. No one chooses π. In flat space, any edge drawn evenly around a point produces it, at every size. It also appears with no geometry at all, from counting two-way choices (above). In HMR it is the signature of the first boundary, the edge of "me".
 It never ends. π is transcendental: its digits never stop and never repeat (Lindemann, 1882, proved it is not the root of any polynomial with whole-number coefficients). The first and simplest limit, a single unit distance tested in every direction, contains a number that cannot be written down completely. In HMR's reading, that is the trace of phase 1: the unlimited is still present inside the first limited thing.
 It is everywhere. π appears wherever something spreads evenly from a point: in every wave and vibration, in the law of gravity and the law of electric force (the 4π of a sphere's surface), in Einstein's equations (8π), in quantum mechanics (ħ = h/2π), and in the bell curve of chance. HMR reads every appearance as a trace of the original boundary, carried into everything built on it.
 One line, five constants
 eiπ + 1 = 0
 Euler's identity joins the five most fundamental numbers in mathematics: 0, 1, π, e, and i. In HMR's terms it is a single sentence about the beginning: nothing (0), the first unit (1), the first boundary (π), growth (e), and a new independent direction (i, the square root of −1, which turns a line into a plane).
 What π could translate to
 If π is the ratio between reach and boundary, it is also a measure of how much "me" there is for each step of reach: the edge of self grows π times faster than its width. HMR proposes that this ratio, between what a being can reach and the boundary it must hold, is a basic quantity of every self, from the first sphere to a living body.

## Seven famous problems, reframed.
 In 2000 the Clay Mathematics Institute named seven of the hardest open problems in mathematics and offered a million dollars for each (Clay Mathematics Institute). One has been solved. If HMR is right, mathematics is UI's ordered thought, and that changes what each problem is really asking. Below, each problem as mathematics frames it, as HMR frames it, and the answer HMR predicts.
 Why can HMR say anything here when mathematicians cannot yet? Because it takes a position on what mathematics is: the ordered thought of UI, built up from a first unit. A position like that implies which way certain questions should fall, in the way a physical theory implies which experiments should succeed. A prediction is not a proof, and each problem below carries its label. Version 2 made no progress on any of these. The predictions are analogies from HMR's premises, with no chain of proof behind them.
### Riemann hypothesis
Open since 1859
 As mathematics frames itThe prime numbers look scattered, but their distribution is governed by the zeros of one function, the zeta function. Are all of its meaningful zeros on a single straight line?
 As HMR reframes itPrimes are the units of counting that cannot be built from smaller repetitions: the irreducible thoughts of arithmetic. The question asks whether their seeming disorder is governed by perfect balance.
HMR predicts: yes. Order underlies every apparent disorder that arises within ordered thought, and the line is that balance. HMR conjecture
### P versus NP
Posed 1971
 As mathematics frames itIf an answer can be checked quickly, can it always be found quickly?
 As HMR reframes itChecking follows an ordered path from a given answer. Finding means searching among possibilities with no order yet, the condition of phase 1. The question asks whether order can always be produced as cheaply as it can be recognized.
HMR predicts: no, P is not NP. Imposing order on the unordered is the costly step, and UI's whole history is that cost. HMR conjecture
