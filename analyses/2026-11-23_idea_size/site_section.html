How big is HMR, by its own measure

The mathematics of thought gives a way to measure an idea: count the pairs of claims that are joined by a proof or by a measurement. We turned it on HMR itself. HMR is not the largest idea today, and we report the count as it came out.

How we counted. We cut the Math page, the Physics page, and the HMR page into 250 single claims and 382 links. A link is certified when the page shows a proof, a computer check, or an established result with its source. It is asserted when the page labels it an HMR reading. It is open when the page labels it a conjecture or in progress. The size R is the number of claim pairs in which one rests on the other. No score was added and nothing was weighted. exact in HMR for the counting rule. The cutting was done by one reader, once, and a finer cut would give larger numbers.

Our first premise certifies nothing yet

“Reality is the thinking of one Universal Intelligence” has no certified consequence in the graph. Everything proved so far rests on the element and the relation. The premise enters only through readings. Fascia first is in the same position, and on these three pages no link ties it to the rest.

HMR has a clash at its own roots

Our rule for the next thought and our assumption that a large pattern is a space cannot both hold (Brightwell and Georgiou, 2010). By our own result, the two branches can only sit side by side. No certified claim rests on both, so nothing proved is lost. The join is lost: up to 832 pairs, about 70% of all that HMR holds today.

A tree with some bracing, not a line

One false certified link costs 5 relations at the median and 132 at the worst. Seventy-two claims have two or more direct supports. We wrote above that HMR's narrative is close to a line. The graph does not show that.

On certified pairs HMR is below every scientific and axiomatic idea in the table, by a factor of roughly ten to a thousand. It is above the worldviews, because it has proofs and they have very few. When readings are counted too, HMR is the same order of size as the worldviews. HMR also spends more assumptions for what it has certified than the mature theories do.

Physics as a whole is not in the table, because it is not one idea. General relativity and quantum theory clash at their roots (the three claims), so they cannot be joined. Each keeps its full size. What is lost is the join, and by our estimate the join would hold more pairs than either theory holds alone. That is how an inconsistency holds a larger model back: the parts stay whole, and the largest term never appears. exact in HMR for the rule. an estimate for the sizes. Mathematics as a whole is a discipline too, since rival foundations cannot be joined. Mathematics on one set of axioms is one idea, and by this measure it is the largest we know of. Its range is patterns themselves, and it fills none of the six slots.

What would make HMR the largest

Three open links carry most of the gain: a rule of growth that yields space (+335 pairs), a law with a memory in one place, which would derive “about” (+213), and a quantum rule for births (+193). The first also removes the clash at our roots. If Einstein's equations and quantum theory both followed, by certified links, from one consistent set of HMR's starting claims, everything those theories hold would rest on HMR's roots, and HMR would hold the join that physics cannot. It would then be the largest idea about reality in this table. HMR conjecture None of the three links exists today.
