# HMR physics paradigm.
 HMR keeps every equation physics has confirmed. What it changes is the framework the equations are read in: space, time, and matter are not the bottom layer of reality, Universal Intelligence (UI) is. Read that way, physics' open problems change shape. This page sets them side by side, then makes a serious attempt at the hardest one, gravity.

## Version 2, November 23, 2026
### What was wrong with Version 1
It described thoughts and how they pile up, not thinking. Nothing in it could be true, false, or a lie, and it had no emotion and no memory. It also read one rule of steady growth as a candidate for inflation without testing whether what grows is a space.
### What Version 2 adds
Definitions of truth, mistakes, lies, and emotion. An exact answer to what one false part does to an idea, and what a contradiction costs, derived and not scored. Proofs checked by computer on every small pattern. Two negative results: Version 1's rules cannot remember, and no rule we tested holds a dimension. One positive: rules that copy shapes do remember.
### What we are trying to accomplish
One mathematics, built only from thoughts and the act of thinking, that gives a simple answer for matter, space, gravity, energy, time, and thought, and that is tested against what science has measured.
### Where we are falling short
How a thought comes to be “about” another is still open. The definitions are proved for single thoughts, not for human minds. No rule yet grows space, holds a memory in one place, or makes a particle. We predict no number that physics lacks, and we have made no progress on any famous open problem.

### The rule of growth, tested as physics
### What it gets right
Thoughts in an order, with no meaning to the birth order of unrelated ones, is the structure physicists call a causal set. Counting thoughts gives volume, and the longest chain gives time. The classical rule for growing one is exact and checked.
established
### What it does not give
Space. For every choice of its free numbers the classical rule grows something long in time with no spatial structure in the large. In our runs, a true four-dimensional scatter measures 3.96 to 3.99 dimensions at every size; grown patterns wander between about 1.4 and 4.2 and settle on none.
established theorem. Version 2 repeated the runs with three to five seeds on eleven rules and settings, including rules that see shape, and none settles on a dimension (the dimension result).
### Steady exponential growth
When each new thought builds on K earlier thoughts, chosen evenly from all of history, the number of thoughts side by side grows as erh after h steps, with r = 0.30, 0.16, 0.088, 0.042, 0.020, 0.0081 and 0.0041 per step for K = 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50, and 100. The reason is counting: the longest chain then grows only as the logarithm of the number of thoughts, and the same count gives a rate of at least 1/(eK). In the simplest rule, where each earlier thought is built on with a small chance p, the number side by side rises by about ln(1/p) e-folds in one burst and then holds steady. The two rules together give steady growth for 10 to 13 e-folds and then an end. Whether this growth is inflation is a conjecture. Version 2 tested whether it is space, and it is not: the measured dimension rises without limit (the dimension result).

### The road to quantum mechanics
 Quantum theory sits exactly one rung above ordinary chance, and HMR's rule of growth climbs that rung when its numbers are allowed to be complex.
### The rung above chance
Ordinary chance obeys one rule: the chance of “this or that” is the sum of the two. Quantum theory breaks that rule for pairs and keeps it for triples. Two paths interfere; three paths add nothing new (Sorkin, 1994). Measured with light through three slits: three-path interference is under one hundredth of two-path (Sinha and others, 2010).
established
### HMR's rule, one rung up
Let the strength p in the simplest rule of growth be a complex number. Pairs of histories then interfere and triples do not, for every pattern the task force has counted. With three thoughts and p = (5 + i√11)/6, “three in a row” has weight 1, “two in a row and one apart” has weight 1, and “one or the other” has weight 0. No rule of chance can do that. This rule is a toy: it is not well defined at large size (task force record, November 20, 2026).
exact in HMR
### Linked outcomes, up to nature's limit
On the standard test of linked outcomes, ordinary chance scores at most 2. A rule one rung up, whose weights stay positive in the strong sense, scores at most 2√2 = 2.83, the limit of quantum theory. Without that condition the score reaches 4, which nature does not (Craig and others, 2007). The task force recomputed all three: 2.000, 2.828, 4.000.
established So UI's rule carries that condition: HMR reading

## Version 1's rules cannot remember, and rules that copy can
 In every rule of Version 1's family, the shape of what is thought later is independent of the shape of what was thought earlier. Nothing later can be a record of anything earlier.
 Why. In those rules a new thought picks a set of earlier thoughts to attend to, weighted only by the set's size and evenly among sets of that size. Whether one late thought rests on another late thought then depends only on late thoughts. The early shape never enters.
 exact in HMR proved by hand. numerical Grow 24 thoughts 200,000 times and compare an early block of births with a later one: the correlation between early and late shape is 0.002 and 0.000 for two rules of the family, against a noise level of 0.002. An earlier check of four rules at 20,000 runs each gave the same. The Math page already said the family is blind to shape. This is sharper: it is blind to its own past.
### What that rules out
 Memory. No pattern is a record of an earlier one.
 A recurring idea. Nothing returns except by chance.
 A particle. A particle would be a shape that persists. These rules cannot hold one.
 “About”. A thought cannot carry information about an earlier pattern, so truth cannot be earned from the law (the mechanics of thought).
 Memory, a lasting particle, and reference are one requirement on the law: it must favor the return of a shape. So the law must see shape. Rules that do are tested below, and the ones that copy shapes remember.

### What none of these rules produced
A memory that lasts and can be rewritten. Two stable states with a barrier between them. A shape that carries itself forward. A steady number of dimensions. The copy rules below change the first three in part, and not the fourth.
### The cost was higher than Version 1 expected
Version 1 proposed giving up one of its four conditions, the third. Every shape-seeing rule we built by hand broke two: the third, and the second, that the birth order of unrelated thoughts means nothing. The one form that keeps the second showed only a faint hint of memory at 24 thoughts and nothing detectable at 200.
### What was missing: copying
The rules in this table that remembered stored a number, such as how wide or how dense the pattern is, not a shape. So we tried rules in which an existing shape raises the chance of the same shape later. Those do remember. The copy rules ↓
### Copy rules: memory found
 Each thought has a small shape: what it is built on directly. In a copy rule, a new thought is more likely to take a shape that already exists. We tried eight such rules and settings, on patterns of up to 128,000 thoughts. numerical
### Copying gives memory
The rules that copy a shape directly all remember it strongly. The agreement between early and late counts of a shape ran from about 0.4 to above 0.9, where Version 1's rules give about zero and the best rule in the table above gave 0.16. Simple copying has no clock: what fades depends on how many times older the pattern has grown, not on how many births have passed.
### Shapes carry themselves forward
When a new thought copies the shape of the thought it builds on, the same shape runs on in lines of descent, far beyond chance, and the longest line grows as the pattern grows. This is the first lasting shape in any of our runs. It is not yet a particle: nothing protects a line from ending, and we have not measured whether a line stays narrow.
### Local copying freezes, it does not store
With copying that looks only nearby, each run settles early into its own mix of shapes and keeps it. A later attempt to write a different mix leaves only a small trace. That is a record of how the run began, not a memory that can be rewritten.
### A two-way bit, but only one for the whole pattern
A true bit appeared in one kind of rule: the new thought favors the shape most common among the latest births. Then there are two stable states. A short write relaxes back, a write past a threshold sticks, and it can be switched both ways. The bit lives in ongoing activity that keeps remaking it. It is one store for the whole pattern, it is not in any place, and the rule reads the order of births to keep it.
### No dimension
Every copy rule still drifts: the measured dimension keeps rising as the pattern grows, as it does without copying. Memory has arrived. Space has not.

## Curved spacetime, from the first distance.
 On the Defining Universal Intelligence page, the mathematics runs from the first distance to flat spacetime, the spacetime of special relativity. The next step is curved spacetime: gravity. This is HMR's route, written out step by step. It does not invent new physics. It walks a road physicists already know, the thermodynamics of spacetime (Jacobson, 1995), with the pieces of causal set theory, and shows where HMR's first distance fits into it.
 The result is a complete chain to Einstein's equations, with every link labeled. Some links are established physics. Some follow from HMR. Some are assumptions HMR has to add, and three are still open. None of this proves Universal Intelligence; mathematics alone cannot do that. What it does is take the idea as far as careful mathematics can go today, and mark exactly where trained physicists have to take over.

### 1. The first distances are scattered, not a grid HMR-derived
 Each act of distinction is an element; each relation says which came before which. Space-time volume is the count of elements: N elements fill a volume V = N ℓ₀⁴. Could the elements sit on a regular grid? HMR's own counting rules that out. On a cubic grid, a surface with direction n is crossed by
 (|nx| + |ny| + |nz|) / ℓ₀²  relations per unit area,
 which varies by up to a factor of √3 with direction, so the strength of gravity would depend on which way you faced. It does not. If the relations carry no structure beyond themselves HMR assumption, they are scattered at random, and a random scattering picks out no direction and no moving frame (Bombelli, Henson, and Sorkin, 2009) standard. That gives relativity's single light cone, with c = ℓ₀ / t₀, and it predicts no change in the speed of light with energy, which matches gamma-ray burst observations (Vasileiou et al., 2013).
### 2. What a boundary counts, and the size of the first distance HMR assumption calculated
 A boundary of area A is crossed by γ relations per ℓ₀², each carrying information s. Its entropy is
 S = η A,   η = s γ / ℓ₀²
 γ can be calculated. Count the pairs of elements that straddle the boundary with nothing in between, the "horizon molecules" of causal set theory. For randomly scattered elements in four dimensions the answer is exact (Barton et al., 2019), and we reproduced it by direct numerical integration (0.17320):
 γ = √3 / 10 ≈ 0.173
 If each crossing relation is one binary distinction, "me" or "not me", then s = ln 2. Black holes fix η = 1 / (4 ℓP2) (Bekenstein, 1973), so
 ℓ₀ = 2 √(s γ) · ℓP  ≈ 0.69 ℓP ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻³⁵ m
 This settles the old question of the factor of 1/4: it is not a separate mystery but the statement of how large the first distance is, a little under one Planck length. Note: this trades G for ℓ₀ rather than predicting G. Counting one natural unit of information per relation instead of one bit gives 0.83 Planck lengths, and one bit per smallest area of a horizon gives 1.7.
### 3. The temperature standard
 An accelerating observer finds empty space warm, at the Unruh temperature
 T = ħ κ / 2π
 This follows exactly from relativity's symmetry plus quantum theory (Bisognano and Wichmann, 1975; Unruh, 1976). HMR does not derive quantum theory; it borrows ħ, positive energy, and locality HMR assumption.
### 4. Coherence displaces distance, as an equation HMR assumption
 This is HMR's central physical claim, now given a number. When energy crosses a boundary, the entropy it carries is recorded as new crossing relations:
 s · ΔN = δQ / T  ⟹  ΔN = −(2π / ħ s) ∫ λ Tab ka kb dλ dA
 For s = ln 2, that is about 9 new relations for every ħ of energy times distance crossing the boundary. This is the step where gravity enters.
### 5. Curvature is the acceleration of the relation count standard
 Follow a bundle of light rays and let n be the number of relations crossing it. Raychaudhuri's equation (1955) says how the bundle's cross-section changes, and in HMR terms that reads:
 Rab ka kb = − d² ln n / dλ²
 Curvature, the thing Einstein's equations describe, is how fast the count of first distances speeds up or slows down along a ray of light.
### 6. Einstein's equations HMR-derived
 Steps 4 and 5 must agree for every light ray through every point. That forces
 Gab + Λ gab = (8π G / c⁴) Tab,   with  G = c³ ℓ₀² / (4 ħ s γ)
 once matter's energy is conserved (the Bianchi identity does the rest) standard. Two things come out that HMR said in words before. Gravity is set by how many first distances a surface holds. Any energy spread evenly through empty space drops out of the equations, so in this route the enormous vacuum number drops out. That empty space carries no weight is HMR's conjecture. HMR conjecture All of this holds only if the order of thoughts is a space, which no rule yet delivers. Λ, the cosmological constant, appears as a free constant, which opens the door to step 8.
### 7. In progress in progress
 Smooth spacetime from discrete relations. No rule for adding relations, in HMR or in any discrete approach to gravity, has been shown to produce a smooth, four-dimensional spacetime. Most random orderings look nothing like space. This is one of the central unsolved problems of quantum gravity.
 Which count is the entropy. Step 2 calculates γ for one definition of "crossing". Why that count, and not another, should be the entropy is not settled; one numerical study of entanglement, in a simplified one-space-plus-one-time model, found a volume law rather than an area law until near-zero modes are removed.
 Quantum theory itself. The temperature in step 3 relies on it. Deriving it is the task force's first goal.
 Where this leaves us
 This page has a chain of equations from the first distance to Einstein's gravity: the old question of the 1/4 answered, the grid ruled out, the number of crossing relations calculated, and HMR's claim about empty space turned into a result. The route reproduces known gravity. Going beyond it is what the task force is working on.
 What remains is not a list of small next steps. It is the same wall every theory of quantum gravity stands in front of: showing that discrete pieces must add up to smooth space-time, and finding where quantum theory comes from. HMR goes about as far with the idea of Universal Intelligence as mathematics can go before it needs physicists working on it full time. Proving the source of reality mathematically will take many people. This page is an invitation to them: every assumption is named, so every one can be attacked, tested, or improved. ## Gravity may not be the kind of thing that is quantized
 Physics has tried for decades to give gravity a quantum description, the way it did for the other forces. Perhaps the clue is that there is none to find, because gravity is a different kind of thing. HMR is not bound to the question as physics frames it. It is bound to what is measured.
 In HMR's mathematics there are two kinds of object. A pattern inside the order of thoughts is counted: it occurs zero times, once, twice. The order itself is not counted. There is one of it, and its properties, such as volume and curvature, are averages over enormous numbers of thoughts. Counts come in whole steps. Averages over N parts look smooth, with a leftover wobble of about one part in √N. exact in HMR these two statements are close to definitions.
 The conjecture. The forces are patterns within the order, which is why they come in quanta. Gravity is not a field living on the order, so there is no field there to quantize. It is how the order behaves on average at very large counts, as pressure is how a gas behaves. Quantizing Einstein's equations would then be like quantizing the gas law: the wrong level. HMR conjecture
 Where this comes from
 HMR's route to Einstein's equations uses the mathematics of Jacobson's derivation, in which the equations are an equation of state, like the gas law (Jacobson, 1995). We take the mathematics as a clue to what gravity is.
### What the conjecture does not say
 It does not say gravity is classical. HMR has one element and one relation, so matter and geometry are made of the same thoughts. If matter can be in two places at once, then either the order can be in two shapes at once, or it cannot and something else carries the two-places-at-once. Those are the two forms the conjecture can take.
### An open question: which of two forms, and the experiment that decides
### Form 1: one kind of thing
 The order itself is undecided between records. Gravity is not a quantized field, and it is not classical either.
 It predicts: two small masses can become entangled through gravity alone.
 Which claim it gives up: that geometry is definite (claim A below).
 It keeps HMR's one element and one relation as they are. open
### Form 2: a definite order with something riding on it
 Every thought is definite, and what behaves in a quantum way is an inner state carried by the thoughts. This is close to Jonathan Oppenheim's proposal of classical gravity with quantum matter and unavoidable noise (Oppenheim, 2023).
 It predicts: no entanglement through gravity. In its place, a trade: the longer a mass can stay in two places at once, the more the geometry must jitter (Oppenheim, Sparaciari, Šoda, and Weller-Davies, 2023).
 Which claim it gives up: that matter itself is in two places at once at every size (claim C below). The order of thoughts stays definite.
 It asks for an inner state on a thought, which HMR's mathematics does not have yet. open
 The test. A proposed experiment holds two small masses near each other, each in two places at once, and looks for entanglement between them that only gravity could have carried (Bose et al., 2017). Entanglement seen: form 1. Entanglement ruled out at the needed sensitivity: form 2. Tests of the noise trade-off in form 2 are described in the decoherence and spacetime diffusion paper above and in a gravitational-wave test (2026). Which form is right is open, and we are still working on it. If form 2 holds, HMR's noise would come from counting, with a size of about one in √N and a grain of volume.
 What would count against the conjecture itself: a grown pattern in which geometric quantities do not settle down as the count rises, or particles that need an inner state that is not a pattern. Neither test can be run until a rule grows space.

## Relativity against quantum theory, as three claims
 The clash between physics' two great theories can be written as three short claims. Any two of them can hold together. All three cannot. The claims belong to the frameworks of physics, not to its measurements, and HMR does not have to hold all three.
 A
### Geometry is definite
Every pair of events is either related or apart, with one answer. This is the root of general relativity.
 B
### Geometry follows matter
Where the mass is fixes which pairs of events are related. This is Einstein's equations.
 C
### Matter can be in two places at once, at every size
A mass can be here and there together, with no upper limit on the mass. This is the root of quantum theory.
 The clash. Put a mass here and there at once (C). Then the geometry is the here-geometry and the there-geometry at once (B). Some pair of events is related in one and apart in the other. A forbids that. exact in HMR as logic, given the three claims as stated. This is the second kind of contradiction on the Math page: one pair claimed both related and apart.
 Each pair is a working theory. A and B: general relativity with ordinary matter. C alone: quantum theory on a fixed background. A and C with B set aside: quantum fields in curved spacetime. Any way of joining them has to give up or weaken at least one claim. Oppenheim's proposal (Oppenheim, 2023) keeps the geometry definite.
 Where the clash lives. By position, all three claims are foundations. By what has been tested, they are leaves. Two-places-at-once has been seen for molecules beyond 25,000 atomic mass units, about 4 × 10⁻²³ kg (Fein et al., 2019). The gravity of a source has been measured down to gold spheres of about 90 milligrams, about 10⁻⁴ kg (Westphal et al., 2021). Between the two lie more than eighteen powers of ten in mass, and the two claims are not tested together there. So the measurements do not conflict. The frameworks do.
 What the mathematics says the contradiction costs
 For the tested body of physics: little. No tested result rests on the part of a claim that is in doubt.
 For everything that needs all three in full: the whole join. The inside of a black hole, the first instant, the weight of empty space, and any single root for all of physics. Each theory keeps its full size. What is lost is what would rest on both. exact in HMR given the definitions on the Math page applied to physics: HMR reading
### HMR does not have to hold all three
 HMR starts from thoughts and their order, not from a geometry with matter placed in it. So it is free to give up one of the three, and each form of its gravity conjecture does (above).
